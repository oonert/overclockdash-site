Which trucks does it fit?

3rd gen 4Runner (1996 to 2002), 1st gen Tacoma (1995 to 2004), T100 (1993 to 1998). In the works: 1996 and 1997 Land Cruiser and Lexus LX 450, and 1st and 2nd gen 4Runner (no engine pages). Want one of those? Say so on the interest form.

How much is it?

We expect $300 to $350. The final price depends on the size of the first batch, which depends on how many people join the list. Everyone on the list hears the final price first.

How hard is it to install?

Factory clock out, OVERCLOCK in, same connector, same opening. Then route the knob cable and the GPS antenna, plus any temp probes. The Bluetooth OBD2 dongle plugs into the diagnostic port. No cutting or splicing.

Can I put the factory clock back?

Yes. It plugs right back in.

Is it 3D printed?

Yes. At our volumes, injection molding doesn't make sense. The case is carbon fiber reinforced, high-heat ABS, made for a truck dash in summer.

Does it work on trucks older than 1996?

Yes, everything except engine codes and live engine data. Those need the 1996 and newer diagnostic port. Some 1995s have it.

Do I need the Bluetooth OBD2 dongle?

Only for engine codes and engine data. One comes in the box. Most Bluetooth Low Energy dongles work. Older Bluetooth 3.0 "classic" dongles don't.

Is it like a ScanGauge?

A ScanGauge shows engine data on its own screen. OVERCLOCK replaces the factory clock, reads the same engine data, and adds GPS, temperature probes, tilt, full logging and full-screen alerts. Full comparison.

Can't I just build this myself with an ESP32?

Yes, and you should. It's a fun project, and you get it exactly your way. OVERCLOCK is for people who'd rather install it this weekend: it fits the factory connector and opening, the case is made for a hot dash, it sleeps without draining your battery, and it's been tested in real trucks.

Can I write my own firmware for it?

Yes. OVERCLOCK is built to run your own firmware. An Arduino library for the board is on the way, and you can always go back to stock.

Why is it called OVERCLOCK FIELD COMPUTER?

Overclocking means pushing hardware past what it was built to do. This pushes a truck's clock a long way past telling time. It also sounds like overlanding, which is where it's meant to go. FIELD COMPUTER is a nod to Toyota's Field Monitor, the trail gauge pod from the same era.

Why a knob and not a touchscreen?

A two-inch touchscreen is hard to hit while driving. One knob: turn, click, hold. Eyes stay on the road, and the screen is all information, no buttons. Flush mount or stick-on housing, five-foot cable.

Can it tune or reprogram my truck?

No. Read-only. The only change it makes is clearing codes, and only when you hold the knob to confirm.

Is there a subscription or an account?

No subscription, no account, no cloud.

Do I need my phone?

No. Everything works from the knob. Your phone is optional: settings, updates and log downloads over Wi-Fi. A companion app is coming.

Are temperature probes included?

Two in the box, up to four total. Plus a trans temp sender and an oil or fuel pressure sender. See the supported senders.

Can I read transmission temperature?

Yes. From OBD2 on 1999 and newer automatics, or from the optional trans temp sender.

Does it work with my solar setup?

Victron SmartSolar charge controllers and SmartShunt battery monitors. Bluetooth lithium batteries: see below.

Which Bluetooth lithium batteries work?

OVERCLOCK is built to read the battery-management systems inside most Bluetooth lithium batteries. Brand-by-brand testing is underway. Send us your battery's Bluetooth name and we'll confirm before you buy.

  • Encryption on in the battery's app? Turn it off.
  • One connection at a time: the clock and your phone app take turns.
  • Some batteries go quiet with no load. The clock shows NOT HEARD until something turns on.

Will it drain my battery?

No. About 110 mA while driving, about one dash bulb. Parked, it's fully off between check-ins: under 1 mA. A low-battery cutoff (11.8 V by default) stops the check-ins. Full numbers on the power page.

What happens when the truck is off?

It saves your trip and switches off. Then it wakes on a schedule you pick: every hour, 30 minutes or 15 minutes. Screen stays off. Each wake lasts about 20 seconds: GPS, probes, barometer and solar, one line in the log, then off. About 0.6 mAh per wake, or 15 mAh a day on the hourly default. Your truck's own parked draw is 500 to 1,200 mAh a day.

What if I disconnect the battery?

Settings and logs are kept. GPS resets the time on your next drive.

Is the screen readable in daylight?

Yes. Bright, high contrast, and auto dimming with the headlights or at sunset.

How do I get updates?

Over Wi-Fi from your phone, or from the memory card. Every update is checked before install.

What memory card should I use?

An 8 GB card comes in the box. Replacing it? A high-endurance microSD made for dash cams, up to 32 GB, from a trusted seller. Knockoff and counterfeit cards can work fine in a computer and still fail in OVERCLOCK, which writes to the card every second.

Where does my data go?

The memory card in the dash. Nowhere else. See the privacy page.

Is it waterproof?

No.

What's the warranty and return policy?

Three-year limited warranty on materials and workmanship. Defects in that time: repaired or replaced. Dead on arrival: replaced, shipping covered. Damage from installation, water or modifications isn't covered. 30-day returns in original condition. Full terms on the terms page.

Is it legal to use?

Yes. Its radio modules carry their own FCC ID. See Regulatory Information. Treat it like your radio: adjust it only when it's safe, keep your eyes on the road, and follow your local distracted-driving laws.
