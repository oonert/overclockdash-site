ONE KNOB, EVERY PAGE

Rotate to move between things. Click to act on the thing you are looking at. Hold to go back. Every screen works the same way, and that is the whole manual.

Four gauges, picked on the spot

Hold on the GAUGES card and the boxes become pickers: speed, rpm, coolant, intake, volts, altitude, heading and whatever else your truck reports. Click one and it opens full size.

One gauge, big

A retro instrument pane: big value, sweep ruler, moving pointer. Colour follows the value, green when healthy, amber warm, red wrong.

Health: is anything slowly getting worse?

Resting volts, crank dip, charging, fuel trim, coolant and mileage as graphs of recent drives. Each gives a verdict in plain words and what to check first.

Four probes, each with a job

Outside, inside, fridge, freezer, engine, cargo, trailer or water. The fridge is watched for you: warm goes amber, two hours warm goes red, frozen lettuce goes ice blue.

Weather from the barometer

Three days of pressure, the trend, and a storm notice when it falls a storm's worth in three hours.

Tilt and camp

Pitch and roll led by the gyro, so a hard stop does not read as a slope. The CAMP view shows the inches of block each wheel needs to level a rooftop tent.

Solar and the house battery

Watts in, amps, charge state, over Bluetooth from a Victron controller and shunt.

Trips, fuel and true MPG

Two trip meters, the tank, range, and a fill-up prompt that learns where you buy fuel. Every mile and every drive go to the card.

Starter: the battery, weeks ahead

Resting volts before each start, how low the crank dips, what the alternator holds. The startup screen says WAIT, then START, so every crank is measured against a true resting number.

The retro clock, with shows

The factory clock redrawn from caliper measurements. Its three buttons work, and five seconds after you stop playing with them the tube puts on a show and lands on the real time.

WHAT IT WATCHES FOR

It is on whenever the key is on and it is watching the whole time. A full-screen takeover with a marching hazard border is for the things that cannot wait; everything else stays out of your way.

  • Overheat. The real coolant number, not the factory gauge that sits dead centre until you are already in trouble.
  • Over-voltage. The highest priority and the only alert with no waiting: a failed regulator is cooking the battery while you watch.
  • Not charging. The alternator quit; the volts tell it before the lights dim.
  • Trouble codes. STOP ENGINE and STOP SOON codes take the screen, with the code in plain words. Lesser codes only light the small lamp.
  • Fridge and freezer. Warm, freezing or thawing, after ten minutes, with how long it has been that way.
  • Storm. Pressure falling a storm's worth in three hours.
  • And every alert fails to off. If a sensor goes quiet, its alert disarms instead of crying wolf. Quieting one is a three-second hold, never a slip.

WHAT IT WILL NOT DO

Trust is built here, not in the feature list.

  • It does not tune or reprogram anything. It reads. The one thing it ever writes is clearing fault codes, and only when you arm it and hold.
  • It never guesses. If a sensor has not answered, the screen says so instead of showing a number that looks real.
  • No cloud, no account, no subscription, no phone app required. Your data lives on a card in your dash.
  • It does not record your position unless you let it. The switch stops the dash writing it at all; open the card and the column is empty.
  • No live engine data on pre-1996 trucks. Engine data needs OBD2, which arrived in the 1996 model year. Older trucks get everything else, and the engine pages simply are not in the rotation.
