FACTORY FIT

Drops into the same spot as the factory clock. Same mounting points, same connector. You can put the original clock back whenever you want.

Install photos All photos

  1. Pull the factory clock. It comes out of the dash the same way it went in.
  2. Drop OVERCLOCK in. OVERCLOCK uses the same opening and the same connector, with no cutting or splicing.
  3. Run the knob cable and GPS antenna, plus any probes you add. The OBD2 dongle plugs into the diagnostic port under the dash.
  • Bluetooth OBD2 dongle included. Plug it in once and leave it there.
  • Everything is controlled with one knob. Turn, click or hold, so your eyes stay on the road.
  • Mount the knob flush with one hole, or stick it on with the included housing and tape if you don't want to drill.
  • Start with the basics or add up to four temp probes and four wireless temp sensors, a transmission temp sender, and an oil or fuel pressure sensor, or a boost sensor for turbocharged and supercharged trucks. Whatever you add gets logged with everything else.

WHAT IT WON'T DO

  • Read-only. OVERCLOCK never changes the truck's programming. You can clear codes, but only when you confirm it.
  • No cloud, no account, no subscription. Everything stays on the SD card in the dash.
  • No location logging unless you want it. It's one switch.
  • No live engine data before 1996. Engine data needs OBD2 and the dongle.
  • Easy on your battery. Under 1 mA parked between wakes, with a low-battery cutoff built in. We recommend the upgraded dongle: it shuts itself off when parked. The standard one draws a little on its own. The numbers. Which OBD dongle?
  • Not a mechanic. OVERCLOCK gives you a head start, not a repair.

WHY I BUILT THIS

I'm Derek. I've daily driven my 1997 4Runner SR5 since 2003: 410,000 miles, all of the lower 48 states, eight full laps around the country. I know these trucks inside and out.

It started with black ice I didn't see coming and a rooftop tent I could never get level. A lot of what's out there is a universal gauge, put in a new case and wedged in where the clock was. My one rule was factory fit: no changes to the dash, the factory mounting points and plug, and a screen that fills the factory bezel. Nothing like that existed, so I built it.

OVERCLOCK is me and my son, in small batches out of our garage in Vancouver, Washington. We hand-solder the connectors, print the cases, and assemble, test and pack every unit. It's open to tinkering too: a rescue mode and a library for the board let you run your own firmware.
